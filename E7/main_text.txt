               1    Objective
               To assemble and experimentally characterize an NMOS source follower (common-drain ampli-
               fier) biased by an active current-source load constructed from an NMOS current mirror using a
               VDD =10 V laboratory DC source. The specific objectives are:
                  1. Verify the current mirror reference branch and establish a stable ≈ 33–34 mA tail current
                     under the 10 V power supply.
                  2. Measure the large-signal DC transfer characteristic (Vout vs. Vin), determine the linear
                     voltage tracking range, and extract the gate-to-source DC level shift (VGS).
                  3. Investigate operating limits, specifically the triode boundary at low Vin and thermal run-
                     away effects observed in the active current-mirror transistor at higher input voltages.
                  4. Characterize the small-signal AC voltage gain (A ) across a frequency range from 6 Hz to

                     100 kHz.
                  5. Measure the small-signal output resistance (R   ) using capacitively coupled discrete re-
                                                                  out
                     sistive loads and compare the extracted value with weak-inversion theoretical predictions
                     adjusted for the 10 V operating rail.

                Following Section 5 and Section 11 of the laboratory manual, the following experimental and

                10 Conclusion
                The active-loaded NMOS source follower was successfully assembled and experimentally char-
                acterized using the SI9926BDY dual MOSFET. The principal conclusions from this experiment
                are:
                   1. Wide Linear DC Tracking: In the operating range V            =1.5 V to 3.5 V, the circuit
                                                                                 in
                      functioned as a near-ideal voltage follower with a measured slope of 0.990 V/V and a level
                      shift of 0.847 V. Below 1.5 V, the active mirror entered triode conduction, losing current
                      regulation. Above 3.5 V, simultaneous high voltage and high current produced internal
                      Joule heating (P ≈ 0.77 W) in the uncooled SO-8 package, initiating thermal runaway
                      where mirror current surged above 100 mA.
                   2. Wideband Flat Frequency Response: Selecting Vin,DC = 3.0 V established a ther-
                      mally stable bias (I  =41.9 mA) for AC testing. The voltage gain remained remarkably
                                          cs
                      flat (|A | = 0.980 V/V at 1 kHz) from 6 Hz up to 100 kHz with zero high-frequency
                              v
                      roll-off, confirming that the follower’s output pole sits well above the measurement band.
                   3. Low Output Resistance: Capacitively coupled load tests yielded an average output
                      resistance of 1.87 Ω. This validates the theoretical promise of the weak-inversion common-
                      drain configuration to provide very low impedance buffering (R     ≈1/g )evenatmodest
                                                                                     out       m
                      bias currents.
