\section{Common Emitter (CE) Configuration}

\subsection{Theory}
A Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT) is a three-terminal device - emitter (E), base (B), and collector (C) - formed by two back-to-back PN junctions. In normal (active-region) operation, the base-emitter junction is forward-biased and the base-collector junction is reverse-biased. A small base current controls a much larger collector current, giving current gain. 

In the CE configuration, the emitter is common to both input (base-emitter) and output (collector-emitter) loops. The input characteristic ($I_B$ vs $V_{BE}$ at constant $V_{CE}$) resembles a diode's forward characteristic. The output characteristic ($I_C$ vs $V_{CE}$ at constant $I_B$) shows a steep rise in the saturation region followed by a nearly flat active region, whose slight upward slope reflects a finite output resistance. The current gain $\beta=\Delta I_C/\Delta I_B$ is typically 50-300.

\subsection{Circuit Connections and Procedure}
\textbf{Output Characteristics:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Connect the transistor in CE configuration: emitter grounded (common), base current supplied through a 10 k$\Omega$ resistor from the base supply with a microammeter (0-500 $\mu$A) in series, and collector connected through a milliammeter (0-10 mA) to the collector supply.
    \item Connect a voltmeter (0-1 V) across base-emitter and a voltmeter (0-30 V) across collector-emitter.
    \item Set the base supply to give $I_B=20\mu A$ (adjust and confirm on the microammeter) and keep it fixed for this run.
    \item Starting with $V_{CE}=0$ V, increase $V_{CE}$ in steps of 0.5 V up to 2 V, then in steps of 2 V up to 20 V, recording $I_C$ at each step.
    \item Repeat for $I_B=40, 60, 80$, and 100 $\mu$A, tabulating $I_C$ vs $V_{CE}$ for each. Switch off the supply.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Input Characteristics:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Retain the CE circuit. Set $V_{CE}=1$ V and hold it fixed for this run.
    \item Increase $V_{BE}$ in steps of 0.05 V from 0 to 0.4 V, then in steps of 0.02 V up to 0.7 V, recording $I_B$ at each step.
    \item Repeat for $V_{CE}=5$ V and $V_{CE}=10$ V. Switch off the supply.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Calculations from Observation Data}
 
\textbf{Current gain $\beta$:} Using the CE output table at $V_{CE}=4.0$~V (active region, away from the
saturation knee):
\[
\beta_{20\to60\mu A} = \frac{\Delta I_C}{\Delta I_B} = \frac{21.691 - 7.158\ \text{mA}}{40\ \mu A} \approx 363
\]
\[
\beta_{60\to100\mu A} = \frac{34.500 - 21.691\ \text{mA}}{40\ \mu A} \approx 320
\]
\[
\beta_{20\to100\mu A} = \frac{34.500 - 7.158\ \text{mA}}{80\ \mu A} \approx 342
\]
The measured $\beta$ is thus in the range \textbf{320--363}, consistent with the BC547 datasheet range
(110--800 depending on the sample, typically 200--400).
 
\textbf{Output resistance $r_o$:} From the same table, taking $\Delta V_{CE}$ between 4.0~V and 8.0~V at
fixed $I_B$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item At $I_B = 20\ \mu A$: $r_o = \dfrac{8.0-4.0\ \text{V}}{(7.583-7.158)\ \text{mA}} \approx 9.4\ \text{k}\Omega$
    \item At $I_B = 60\ \mu A$: $r_o = \dfrac{4.0\ \text{V}}{(24.970-21.691)\ \text{mA}} \approx 1.22\ \text{k}\Omega$
    \item At $I_B = 100\ \mu A$: $r_o = \dfrac{4.0\ \text{V}}{(40.570-34.500)\ \text{mA}} \approx 0.66\ \text{k}\Omega$
\end{itemize}
$r_o$ decreases as $I_B$ (and hence $I_C$) increases, as expected from the Early-effect slope of the
output family.
 
\textbf{Input resistance $r_i$:} Using the CE input table, in the exponential (forward-conducting)
region between $V_{BE}=0.64$~V and $0.70$~V:
\[
r_{i,\,V_{CE}=1V} = \frac{\Delta V_{BE}}{\Delta I_B} = \frac{0.70-0.68\ \text{V}}{(0.0056-0.0041)\ \text{mA}} \approx 13.3\ \text{k}\Omega
\]
\[
r_{i,\,V_{CE}=5V} = \frac{0.02\ \text{V}}{(0.0057-0.0044)\ \text{mA}} \approx 15.4\ \text{k}\Omega
\]
\[
r_{i,\,V_{CE}=10V} = \frac{0.02\ \text{V}}{(0.0063-0.0047)\ \text{mA}} \approx 12.5\ \text{k}\Omega
\]
Taking the step before that ($V_{BE}=0.64\to0.66$~V) gives $r_i$ in the range $14$--$20\ \text{k}\Omega$
across the three curves, so overall $r_i \approx 13$--$20\ \text{k}\Omega$ near cut-in, decreasing
towards the lower end of that range as $V_{BE}$ increases further (consistent with the diode-like
$I_B$–$V_{BE}$ curve becoming steeper, i.e.\ lower dynamic resistance, at higher forward bias). This is
a moderate input resistance, as expected for CE — higher than CB ($\approx 830\ \Omega$,) but
much lower than CC ($\approx 8.3\ \text{M}\Omega$), matching the expected ordering
$r_{i,CB} < r_{i,CE} < r_{i,CC}$.

 
\section{Common Base (CB) Configuration}

\subsection{Theory}
In the CB configuration, the base is common to both loops. The input characteristic ($I_E$ vs $V_{EB}$ at constant $V_{CB}$) is again diode-like. The output characteristic ($I_C$ vs $V_{CB}$ at constant $I_E$) is extremely flat, since the collector current is set almost entirely by the emitter current and is nearly independent of $V_{CB}$ giving CB circuits a very high output resistance. The current gain $\alpha=\Delta I_C/\Delta I_E$ is always slightly less than 1 (typically 0.95-0.99).

\subsection{Circuit Connections and Procedure}
\textbf{Output Characteristics:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Connect the transistor in CB configuration: base grounded (common), emitter current supplied through a 1 k$\Omega$ resistor from the emitter supply with a milliammeter (0-10 mA) in series, and collector connected through a milliammeter (0-10 mA) to the collector supply.
    \item Connect a voltmeter (0-1 V) across emitter-base and a voltmeter (0-30 V) across collector-base.
    \item Set the emitter supply to give $I_E=2$ mA and hold it fixed for this run.
    \item Starting with $V_{CB}=0$ V, increase $V_{CB}$ in steps of 0.5 V up to 2 V, then in steps of 2 V up to 20 V, recording $I_C$ at each step.
    \item Repeat for $I_E=4, 6, 8$, and 10 mA. Switch off the supply.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Input Characteristics:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Retain the CB circuit. Set $V_{CB} = 0$ V and hold it fixed for this run.
    \item Increase $V_{EB}$ in steps of 0.05 V from 0 to 0.4 V, then in steps of 0.02 V up to 0.7 V, recording $I_E$ at each step.
    \item Repeat for $V_{CB}=5$ V and $V_{CB}=10$ V. Switch off the supply.
\end{enumerate}

\[
\alpha = \frac{\Delta I_C}{\Delta I_E} = \frac{9.96 - 1.99\ \text{mA}}{10 - 2\ \text{mA}} = 0.99625

\[
\beta = \frac{0.99625}{1-0.99625} \approx 266

\textbf{Output resistance $r_o$:} Using the re-measured CB output table, taking the full swept range
$V_{CB}=0$~V to $8.0$~V at each fixed $I_E$:
\[
r_{o,\,I_E=2mA} = \frac{\Delta V_{CB}}{\Delta I_C} = \frac{8.0\ \text{V}}{(2.00-1.99)\ \text{mA}} \approx 800\ \text{k}\Omega

\]
So $r_o$ is in the range \textbf{400--800 k$\Omega$}, roughly two orders of magnitude higher than the
CE-measured $r_o$ (0.66--9.4~k$\Omega$) and even higher than the CC-measured $r_o$
(2.8--7.5~k$\Omega$). This is consistent with CB theory: since $I_C$ is set almost entirely by $I_E$ and
depends only very weakly on $V_{CB}$, the CB output characteristic is the flattest of the three
configurations, giving by far the highest output resistance.
 
\textbf{Input resistance $r_i$:} Using the CB input table, in the forward-conducting region between
$V_{EB}=0.30$~V and $0.35$~V:
\[
r_i = \frac{\Delta V_{EB}}{\Delta I_E} = \frac{0.35-0.30\ \text{V}}{(0.4227-0.3625)\ \text{mA}} \approx 831\ \Omega
\]
\textbf{Cross-check of $\beta = \alpha/(1-\alpha)$:} The CE-measured $\beta$ (320--363) and the
CB-derived $\beta$ from $\alpha=0.99625$ ($\approx 266$) agree to within the same order of magnitude,
which is a reasonable cross-check given how sensitive $\beta=\alpha/(1-\alpha)$ is to small errors in
$\alpha$ near 1.
 
\section{Common Collector (CC) Configuration}

\subsection{Theory}
In the CC configuration (also called an emitter follower), the collector is common to both loops. The output characteristic ($I_E$ vs $V_{EC}$ at constant $I_B$) closely resembles the CE output characteristic, since $I_E \approx I_C$. The input characteristic is normally plotted as $I_B$ vs $V_{BC}$ at constant $V_{EC}$, and falls off as the base-collector reverse bias increases. The current gain $\gamma=\Delta I_E/\Delta I_B \approx \beta+1$, so CC circuits provide current gain close to that of the CE configuration but are used mainly for their high input resistance and low output resistance (voltage buffering).

\subsection{Circuit Connections and Procedure}
\textbf{Output Characteristics:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Connect the transistor in CC configuration: collector grounded (common), base current supplied through a 10 k$\Omega$ resistor from the base supply with a microammeter (0-500 $\mu$A) in series, and emitter connected through a milliammeter (0-10 mA) to the emitter supply.
    \item Connect a voltmeter (0-30 V) across base-collector and a voltmeter (0-30 V) across emitter-collector.
    \item Set the base supply to give $I_B=20\mu A$ and hold it fixed for this run.
    \item Starting with $V_{EC}=0$ V increase $V_{EC}$ in steps of 0.5 V up to 2 V, then in steps of 2 V up to 20 V, recording $I_E$ at each step.
    \item Repeat for $I_B=40, 60, 80$, and 100 $\mu$A. Switch off the supply.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Input Characteristics:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Retain the CC circuit. Set $V_{EC} = 1$ V and hold it fixed for this run.
    \item Increase $V_{BC}$ in steps of 0.5 V from 0 to 10 V, recording $I_B$ at each step.
    \item Repeat for $V_{EC}=5$ V and $V_{EC}=10$ V. Switch off the supply.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Calculations from Observation Data}
 
\textbf{Current gain $\gamma$:} Using the CC output table at $V_{EC}=4.0$~V (with the corrected
$I_B=100\mu A$ entry at $V_{EC}=8.0$~V):
\[
\gamma_{20\to60\mu A} = \frac{\Delta I_E}{\Delta I_B} = \frac{(0.2743-0.0820)\ \text{mA}}{40\ \mu A} \approx 4.8
\]
\[
\gamma_{60\to100\mu A} = \frac{(0.4867-0.2743)\ \text{mA}}{40\ \mu A} \approx 5.3
\]
\textbf{Note:} theory predicts $\gamma = \beta+1$, which using the CE-measured $\beta\approx342$ gives
$\gamma\approx343$, and using the CB-derived $\beta\approx266$ gives $\gamma\approx267$. The values
computed directly from the CC output table ($\approx5$) are roughly two orders of magnitude lower than
either prediction.
 
\textbf{Output resistance $r_o$:} Using the corrected table, between $V_{EC}=6.0$~V and $8.0$~V:
\begin{itemize}
    \item At $I_B = 20\ \mu A$: $r_o = \dfrac{2.0\ \text{V}}{(0.4990-0.0960)\ \text{mA}} \approx 4.96\ \text{k}\Omega$
    \item At $I_B = 60\ \mu A$: $r_o = \dfrac{2.0\ \text{V}}{(0.5947-0.3261)\ \text{mA}} \approx 7.45\ \text{k}\Omega$
    \item At $I_B = 100\ \mu A$: $r_o = \dfrac{2.0\ \text{V}}{(1.324-0.6158)\ \text{mA}} \approx 2.82\ \text{k}\Omega$
\end{itemize}
These values are of a similar order to the CE-measured $r_o$ (0.66--9.4~k$\Omega$), which is expected
since the CC output characteristic closely resembles the CE output characteristic ($I_E\approx I_C$).
This internal consistency with the CE $r_o$ range is a useful sanity check, even though $\gamma$ itself
did not check out above.
 
\textbf{Input resistance $r_i$:} Using the CC input table, between $V_{BC}=3.0$~V and $3.5$~V:
\[
r_{i,\,V_{EC}=1V} = \frac{\Delta V_{BC}}{\Delta I_B} = \frac{0.5\ \text{V}}{(0.3426-0.2825)\ \mu A} \approx 8.32\ \text{M}\Omega
\]
\[
r_{i,\,V_{EC}=5V} = \frac{0.5\ \text{V}}{(0.3500-0.2888)\ \mu A} \approx 8.17\ \text{M}\Omega
\]
\[
r_{i,\,V_{EC}=10V} = \frac{0.5\ \text{V}}{(0.3694-0.3076)\ \mu A} \approx 8.09\ \text{M}\Omega
\]
So $r_i \approx 8.1$--$8.3\ \text{M}\Omega$ across all three $V_{EC}$ settings — a very high input
resistance, as expected for the CC (emitter-follower) configuration, and consistent across the sweep.


\section{Results and Conclusion}
The input and output characteristics of the BC547 BJT were obtained experimentally, and cross-verified
against LTSpice simulation, for the CE, CB, and CC configurations.
 
From the CE data, $\beta$ was measured in the range 320--363 ($\approx342$ average), $r_o$ decreased
from $\approx9.4\ \text{k}\Omega$ at low $I_C$ to $\approx0.66\ \text{k}\Omega$ at high $I_C$ (consistent
with the Early effect), and $r_i$ was found to be $\approx13$--$20\ \text{k}\Omega$ near cut-in.
 
From the CB data, $\alpha=0.99625$ was measured directly, giving a cross-checked
$\beta=\alpha/(1-\alpha)\approx266$ — in reasonable agreement with the CE-measured $\beta$, given the
high sensitivity of that formula to small errors in $\alpha$ near 1. The CB input resistance was
$r_i\approx830\ \Omega$, the lowest of the three configurations, as expected.
 
From the CC data, $r_i\approx8.1$--$8.3\ \text{M}\Omega$ was measured, the highest of the three
configurations and consistent with the CC (emitter-follower) circuit's known use for high-impedance
buffering. The CC output resistance ($\approx2.8$--$7.5\ \text{k}\Omega$) was of similar order to the
CE output resistance, as expected since $I_E\approx I_C$. However, the current gain $\gamma$ computed
directly from the CC output table ($\approx4.8$--$5.3$) did not agree with the theoretical prediction
$\gamma=\beta+1\approx267$--$343$; the recorded $I_E$ values in that table are consistently 10--50 times
smaller than the corresponding CE $I_C$ values at matching $I_B$ settings, pointing to a likely
instrumentation range issue during that specific measurement run rather than a genuine device
inconsistency. 
 
Overall, the relative magnitudes of $r_i$ across the three configurations matched theory exactly
($r_{i,CB}<r_{i,CE}<r_{i,CC}$), and the $\beta$/$\alpha$ cross-check agreed within a reasonable margin.
